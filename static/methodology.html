What makes a great hackathon?

Hackfinder Score estimates whether an event is worth a participant’s time, on a scale of 0–100. It uses published rules and source evidence. It is a guide to discovery, not a promise about your experience.

How it works

Five dimensions are combined using weights for each kind of event. Missing information is estimated from similar events, never treated as zero. Dates and distance belong to sorting and filtering, not to the score.

Cash rewards use a logarithmic scale; non-cash rewards are capped. Community size is compared within the same source and kind. Counts from duplicate listings are never added together.

Confidence and “Not enough info”

Confidence describes how much evidence supports the score: high, medium, or low. Low-confidence badges keep their tier styling and are prefixed with ~. Below the evidence threshold of 30, the badge shows – with “Not enough info”. Unknown does not mean bad.

Open any badge to see dimension values, factual reasons, missing information, and confidence. Low scores use grey numerals and a dashed border.

Why it’s open

Unlike closed scores, ours publishes its formula. You can inspect the inputs, weights, reputation lists, and version, then decide what matters to you.
